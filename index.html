Daka — Market Trend & Strategy Backtest Dashboard
Daka · Portfolio Case Study

An interactive dashboard I built to answer one question: do simple, rules-based technical-analysis strategies actually beat just buying and holding, and does the answer change across markets and timeframes?

Data

OHLCV price data pulled from two public market APIs: Alpha Vantage (S&P 500 ETF, SPY: 100 trading days and 5 years of weekly bars) and the Crypto.com Exchange API (BTC/USDT, 50 hourly bars). Responses are parsed, sorted and validated in the page. This edition runs on a saved snapshot from early November 2026, so every number is reproducible.

Method

Every indicator is computed from scratch in JavaScript, with no charting or finance libraries: SMA/EMA, RSI, MACD, Bollinger Bands and ATR. A rules engine turns them into a trend read and a 6-point signal checklist. Five mechanical strategies (trend following, mean reversion, breakout, MACD momentum, IBS) are then backtested on the same bars and benchmarked against buy-and-hold.

Tools & skills

REST API data collection · data cleaning & parsing · feature engineering · backtesting & performance metrics (win rate, average and compounded return) · custom SVG data visualization · dashboard design.

Key findings

Limitations & next steps
  • Small, in-sample windows. 50 hourly BTC bars and 100 daily SPY bars leave only a handful of trades per rule, so the win rates are noisy. The 5-year weekly series is the most meaningful test.
  • No costs. The backtests ignore fees, slippage and taxes, which would hurt the high-turnover rules the most.
  • Prices aren't dividend-adjusted, so buy-and-hold on SPY is slightly understated.
  • Next: longer histories, an out-of-sample test period, transaction costs, and risk-adjusted metrics (Sharpe ratio, max drawdown).

Put / Call Ratio
Ratio of put contracts to call contracts traded, by expiration. Alpha Vantage's own read on the full chain: ≤0.6 typically bullish positioning, ≥1.0 typically bearish. This describes current options flow only — not a prediction, and not personalized advice.
Volume / Open Interest Ratio
A high ratio means today's volume is large next to existing open contracts — fresher, more speculative activity. A low ratio means most positions are being held rather than traded — usually calmer conditions.
